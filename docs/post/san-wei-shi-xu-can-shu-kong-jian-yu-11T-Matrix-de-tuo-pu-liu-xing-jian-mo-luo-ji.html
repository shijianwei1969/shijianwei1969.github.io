I. Core Positioning: Three-Dimensional Temporality Is a Parameter Space, Not Multiple Physical Times

The most easily misinterpreted aspect of three-dimensional temporality is understanding it as "three physical times flowing simultaneously" or "three parallel worlds." This is a completely erroneous positioning. In the modeling logic of 11T-Matrix, three-dimensional temporality is, from beginning to end, a mathematical parameter space describing topological deformation, rather than perceivable physical time itself.

The truly perceivable physical time is merely the arc-length parameter of a certain timelike curve in this three-dimensional parameter space—the observer's own evolutionary trajectory. The observer can only experience the "present" along this one-dimensional curve, and cannot jump to another temporal direction to experience a different "now." Those extra parameter dimensions are only responsible for describing the overall deformation of the topological body and the global phase order; they do not enter the observer's causal chain, nor do they generate any directly perceivable "time flow."

In short: it is not that "there are three times," but rather that in order to keep the mathematical description of topological evolution continuous and free of singularities, we need a three-dimensional parameter space to carry the deformation; the observer moves along only one one-dimensional path within it, and the scale of this path is the time we experience. This positioning is the premise of the entire modeling framework and the key to avoiding conceptual misunderstandings.

$$ M = \Sigma \times T $$

This is fundamentally different from the standard cosmology paradigm of "space expansion," and corresponds completely to the core physical picture of 11T-Matrix: "space does not move, while the temporal geometry is in motion."

The core mathematical role of three-dimensional temporality is to make changes in topological structure a continuous process, without having to pass through singularities.

1. The Essential Dilemma of One-Dimensional Time

Under the standard one-dimensional time framework, we define a set of topological invariants (such as homotopy groups, Chern classes, mapping degree, etc.) denoted as $Q$, used to characterize the shape of the topological body. The transition of topology from one form $Q_1$ to another form $Q_2$ corresponds to a path in the parameter space. If the parameter space is only one-dimensional (single time), then the path between two different topological classes will inevitably pass through a topologically discontinuous point—that is, a mathematical singularity. This is precisely the deep origin of black hole singularities and cosmological singularities in general relativity: one-dimensional time is insufficient to carry the smooth transition of topology, and evolution inevitably "breaks."

2. The Detour Mechanism of Three-Dimensional Temporality

When the temporal parameter space is extended to three dimensions, the situation undergoes an essential change. The topological invariants become functions of the temporal coordinates $Q(\tau)$; the change of topological shape is equivalent to selecting a continuous path in the three-dimensional temporal space $T$, allowing $Q(\tau)$ to smoothly evolve from $Q_1$ to $Q_2$ along the path. The high-dimensional parameter space provides sufficient detour freedom: the singularity that must be passed through on the one-dimensional path can be bypassed along the side in three-dimensional space. Throughout the entire process, the topological invariants change continuously, the manifold does not tear, no infinities appear, and it is mathematically completely self-consistent.

This is the mathematical essence of "topological root domain replacing singularity": the singularity at the center of a black hole is merely an apparent singularity under one-dimensional time projection; in the complete three-dimensional temporal parameter space, topological evolution is smooth and detouring, and there is no true mathematical singularity.
